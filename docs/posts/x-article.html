What a calibrated yes/no buys a confirm gate on an offline edge agent

Consider a network-management assistant that runs a 4-billion-parameter model on the neural processing unit (NPU) of a Qualcomm QCS9075 board. It turns an operator's sentence into one switch command, and a human approves every write before it reaches the hardware. On canonical phrasing it is a 92 to 97 percent agent. On paraphrases it is a 77 percent agent. The accuracy is not the interesting part; two other properties are.

The misses are structured

Of 44 misses on 195 paraphrased requests, 20 are confusions between adjacent commands: put a port in one VLAN versus make it a trunk, cut PoE power versus shut the interface down. Thirteen are "run diagnostics instead of a status read," which is usually defensible; ten are format slips with the right command in the wrong slot, which a deterministic normalization recovers. The dangerous subset is small in count and large in consequence.

The model emits no probability

A generated tool call is a one-hot event. The confirm card shows the rendered command and the model's own reason, both written by the thing that may have misread the request. And the compiled NPU runtime returns no logprobs, ignores the token limit and keeps no prefix cache (measured on GenieX v0.4.0), so there is nothing to read off the serving stack either.

TypeSafe's Jev reframes this class of decision as typed questions over a state: a yes/no returns one probability, a choice returns a distribution over the options you supplied, an ordered score returns a distribution and an expected level. All questions are answered in one pass, nothing is generated, and the model is trained so the probabilities are calibrated. Jev is cloud-only, and the board has no internet. The question, then: how much of that idea can be served locally, next to the generative agent, and what does it buy the gate?

Four ways to serve one interface

A small library makes four very different backends return the same object per question: a probability vector over the options, the scores it came from, and a concentration confidence. One calibration and analysis pipeline then serves all of them.

  • A next-token readout of the resident 4B: the question and its option legend are the constant system prompt (single-token labels, A to X or Yes/No), the request is the user message, and llama.cpp's server returns the distribution at the first generated position. The prefix cache makes each question cost only the request's tokens. Option order is averaged over three rotations.
  • Three open Jev reproductions, zero-shot: a 151M ModernBERT cross-encoder shipped as ONNX, a 421M RLCD-trained model, a DeBERTa-v3-large with a span-scoring head; plus an ordinary NLI cross-encoder as the floor.
  • A 150M encoder fine-tuned on the domain with a proper-scoring-rule loss, on training groups only.
  • Rules. One-hot, no epistemics; the fallback on a timeout.

What was measured

The workload is the application's own evaluation set (65 canonical requests, 195 style-controlled paraphrases) plus 259 rows written for the study: adversarial confusables, symptom-first diagnostics, documentation and out-of-scope messages, requests for tools outside the production profile. Two question batteries: a router (which tool, which of 23 commands, is it a write, is it about PoE power) and a gate ("does the proposed call do what was asked?"), the latter labeled from the generative model's real calls plus synthetic swaps.

Two things to read off that table. The command accuracy (84.6 percent) is about the generative call's own semantic command accuracy on paraphrases (83.1 percent): the readout is not a better router. And raw next-token confidence is dangerous: 23 of 40 errors sat above 0.9. One fitted temperature per fold, fitted on other seed requests, halves the calibration error and the confident errors; it cannot remove them. On the adversarial rows, reversing the option order flipped 29 to 36 percent of answers; averaging rotations is the cheap fix.

What the gate catches

The gate question with the readout backend, 939 pairs: AUROC 0.95. On synthetic negatives it is nearly airtight: of 156 dangerous swaps it accepted one. On the generative model's real calls it flags 15 of the 20 dangerous confusions and 7 of the 13 diagnose-instead calls at p below 0.5, and false-flags 30 of 151 correct proposals. At that threshold the card carries a warning on three of four dangerous misreads and on one in five correct proposals. Move the threshold and the trade moves with it; the point of the probability is that the trade is now a decision rather than an absence.

Wording matters more than expected. Two phrasings of the same yes/no question put the raw probability on correct proposals at 0.21 and at 0.82. The ranking survived either way; the thresholds did not.

The open reproductions, zero-shot

Fast, honest, and at chance on the gate (AUROC 0.55 to 0.66). The 151M model collapses to one label once a question has more than about six options; the same four probe requests get one answer at twelve. Its authors evaluate on five-candidate tasks. Above that, ask hierarchically or fine-tune.

A fine-tuned 150M encoder

Trained on about 2,600 examples with question-order fixed, it reaches 65 to 70 percent on the router at about 10 milliseconds per battery on a GPU, 5 to 15 points under the 4B readout on identical held-out rows. It answers a question differently when asked alone than when packed with its siblings, because the training layout was fixed. Its gate head learned the class prior. Both are training-recipe problems, and both are cheap to fix relative to a bigger model.

What carries over

  • Ask for a distribution at the gate, not a sentence. A card with a probability has an operating point.
  • A probability you did not calibrate on your own labeled rows is not a probability.
  • Rotate the options. Order bias flips a double-digit share of a small model's multiple-choice answers.
  • Do not route through the model you are checking. A text label from the 4B was worse than its own tool call (88.7 versus 93.3 percent on the tool, 70.8 versus 83.1 on the command).
  • Measure the serving path before designing around logits. On this board, production has none.
